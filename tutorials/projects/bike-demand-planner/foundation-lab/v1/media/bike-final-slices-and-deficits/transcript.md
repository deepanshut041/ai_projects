# Find where the later forecasts still fall short

## Ask which hours carry the largest burden

The overall average hides a much harder slice. We defined working day rush as hours seven, eight, nine, sixteen, seventeen and eighteen before opening the final outcomes. It contains seven hundred and forty one recorded hours, with group mean absolute error two hundred and sixty five point four three. Signed error there is negative two hundred and forty three point eight one. The forest is lower at two hundred and twenty six point seven four, but still misses substantially. These counts and definitions matter to interpretation. Finding a busy hour failure does not establish a new causal model or an operational inventory policy.

## Read month averages with actual coverage

Month slices show different later behavior. July has seven hundred and forty three observations and mean absolute error one hundred and thirty two point one four. September has seven hundred and twenty and error one hundred and sixty four point three four. October has seven hundred and eight recorded hours; December has seven hundred and forty two and lower average error. All six observed month counts reconstruct the final population. January through June final slices are empty, with undefined metrics rather than invented zero error. Month and rush dimensions overlap, so adding their separate counts would double count observations.

## The same deficit can have two denominators

The total underprediction deficit is five hundred and seventeen thousand, nine hundred and forty one point zero zero eight five rentals. Divide by all four thousand, three hundred and seventy five observed hours and the average deficit is one hundred and eighteen point three nine. Divide by only the three thousand, seven hundred and thirty five underpredicted hours and it becomes one hundred and thirty eight point six seven. Both values are correct, with different questions and denominators. They measure misses against observed rentals, not every person who wanted a bicycle; unmet demand is not directly recorded here.

## Keep missing outcomes outside the score

Between the first and last final observations, forty expected naive hourly slots are unrecorded. Their absence does not establish zero rentals, nor does it establish a model error of any known size. We retain the missingness and score only recorded outcomes. October therefore has seven hundred and eight observed hours rather than a complete calendar month of hourly labels. Substituting zeros would change both numerator and denominator, answering a different question without supporting observations. The replay application follows the same principle: an estimate may exist for a valid request while its actual count and residual remain unavailable.

## A large miss reveals a limit, not its cause

The largest retained group miss occurs on September twelfth at eighteen. The model predicts three hundred and sixty seven point four five, while the observed count is nine hundred and seventy seven. Its residual is negative six hundred and nine point five five. Across the final period, mean target is two hundred and fifty four point one one, while mean forecast is one hundred and forty two point seven six. Their difference agrees with signed error. This describes a level gap consistent with old fitted group means. It does not identify why demand changed or establish a causal intervention.

## Nearby misses are not independent experiments

Nearby hours also tend to miss together. Over four thousand, three hundred and sixty nine consecutive observed hour pairs, residual correlation is about zero point eight three four nine. Pairs spanning missing hours are excluded from that adjacency calculation. Daily and Monday start weekly summaries retain their actual counts. The report does not pretend that thousands of adjacent hours are independent future experiments. Its time and slice comparisons are descriptive evidence about this one later period, without an unsupported independent hour confidence interval. A different future operating environment remains an unanswered generalization question rather than something a large row count guarantees.
