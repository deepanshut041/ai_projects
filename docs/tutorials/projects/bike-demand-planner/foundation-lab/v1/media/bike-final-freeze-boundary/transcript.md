## Reject altered evidence; retain poor forecasts

Change a protocol hash, model bytes or raw source and the relevant check rejects the assessment. Reordering an identity also fails rather than pairing a prediction with another hour. Synthetic checks exercise these cases without consuming new real targets. A valid but inaccurate forecast belongs in the result; it is different from broken provenance or alignment. Checksums establish byte identity, while inspected code and execution receipts explain meaning and order. They do not prove the chosen model is scientifically appropriate. On repeated invocation, the example shows the existing assessment instead of silently replacing it.

## What changes after a test-informed decision?

Suppose the final table motivates a different model choice. We can keep the original fixed assessment and report exactly what it found. The next choice is now informed by this test. That can be useful development, provided its history stays visible. A new independent quality claim needs new held out evidence for the revised procedure. Randomly splitting these already inspected outcomes again does not restore their original independence. The point of freezing is to preserve the meaning of the result, including a disappointing one, rather than guarantee that the original choice will always win.
